Common questions

Are these new pet checklists really free?

Yes. Every checklist on this site is free as a printable PDF and as a web worksheet you can tick off on your phone. There is no account, no email signup, and no locked version with the good parts in it.

What should I buy before bringing a new pet home?

Start with the supply shopping list for your species — food and water bowls, the right bedding or litter, a carrier or crate, and a few days of the food they are already eating so you are not switching diets on day one.

What should I do in the first 24 hours with a new pet?

Keep the world small. Set up one quiet space, show them where food, water, and the bathroom are, and let them come to you. The first week home checklist breaks the rest of the week down day by day.

How do I keep track of weights, vet visits, and medications?

That is what the free Your Pet's Health Log is for. Once the first week is behind you, it keeps the running record a vet will actually ask you about — weights, dates, doses, and questions for the next appointment.

Can I use a checklist instead of seeing a vet?

No. These checklists help you get organized and ask better questions. Anything about diet amounts, medication, or a health concern belongs with a vet who works with your pet's species.

Before you print: a quick note

These checklists are meant to help you get organized and ask better questions — they're general starting points, not species-specific medical or husbandry guidance for every situation. A vet who works with your pet's species is the right source for anything about diet amounts, medication, or a health concern.

If something feels urgent — your new pet won't eat, seems in pain, or is acting very differently than usual — contact a vet promptly rather than waiting to work through a checklist.
